My longing for a community started with NSS (National Service Scheme) when I was in 12th grade. Back then, there were seven or eight of us who came back for every event after the seven-day residential camp. But each event had its own charm - a sense of belonging, whether it was a cleaning drive or hosting a competition.

![NSS volunteers after an event](../images/blogs/life-is-good/nss-hss.webp)

Fast forward few months, after the announcement of KEAM result, I was scanning through every government colleges, looking for the one that matched my rank and my vision. One of my main criteria was: "Does it have an active NSS unit?"

And I'm glad to write that I found the right NSS unit. But, something else was missing, which I found out after first few months in the college: There wasn't any community that shared the same passion as me. i.e., a community centered around tech and problem-solving (See: [At Least I Tried](https://justfahad.me/blog/atleast-i-tried) - Nov 2024) - An idea that was instilled in me by [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com/) (A community of builder teens). So, I went on joining (almost) every event, applying to (almost) all execoms. By the end of my 2nd sem - I found one that clicked with me: **TinkerHub GEC Palakkad**.

![TinkerHub GEC Palakkad 2024 core team](../images/blogs/life-is-good/core-team-24.webp)

I was selected as the Co-Lead 2. Although, the execom was nearing the end of its tenure, I spent time with Diya Betcy (the awesome yet humble lead), Gayathri K S (Cheerful & motivating WIT lead), Uvais J (the mysterious one with lots of big plans). There were other prominent members as well, serving in both core team and volunteering team (Shout out to volunteering team - they don't get enough credits). Over the next few months, I collaborated with these people and host some interesting events too. And most notable experience from this tenure was CLS.

![Campus Leadership Summit 2024](../images/blogs/life-is-good/cls-24.webp)

CLS or The Campus Leadership Summit - A gathering of all core team from all TinkerHub campus chapters, hosted in Ernakulam, spanning over 3 days and multiple sessions (each designed to help align and strengthen campus leadership teams), each day concluding in a fun cultural night. CLS gave me an idea of what community could be, I got to hear experiences from teams all over Kerala. I asked doubts, and doubted myself even more - but believe that's how communities work. Its not always perfect, constant setbacks and blockades are expected especially in a college environment.

![A regular TinkerHub event at GEC Palakkad](../images/blogs/life-is-good/normal-event-tinkerhub.webp)

See... The community part in our college isn't really that apparent yet. But, I believe that this is just a phase - an invisible one. You can't see a group sitting around talking about stuff like building things, solving problems, etc. But in between the days of classes, assignments, exams, sports, culturals, etc. - there are some people who asks questions, some share what they know and some cheer on others' success. These people are rare but they are there - struggling to pursue those interests on their own.

By March 2025, applications for TinkerHub campus leadership (Applications for campus leads) opened, and I vividly remember applying for it at midnight while lying on the floor of staff room at MEC Thrikkakara (Weird place, I know but it was during Tink Hack 2.0 and I was exhausted). In my mind, I was like: I could attend selection camp and meet some interesting people.

![TinkerHub Campus Leadership Selection Camp](../images/blogs/life-is-good/selection-camp-25.webp)

Fortunately, I got shortlisted to attend selection camp - which was held at Trivandrum (My proper Trivandrum visit was then) - IT WAS AWESOME, I can recollect almost all moments from that camp. It was the proper "I found my people!" moment. After the camp, we roamed the city and the return train journey became another wonderful memory. But the most surprising part for me was that - I actually got selected as a lead!! (I have already wrote about it in [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/just-fahad_tinkerhub-gecpalakkad-milestone-activity-7349476812801937409-giGH))

Being a lead my first line of action was to select the core team, I prioritised bringing new faces to leading roles - it took some time, needed a lot of judgement but at last, I found myself a super supportive team made up of: Sanjana K (A cheerful creative person - she carried the team), Sreelakshmi J (An underrated person, her way of managing things/sessions are so cool), and other cool people as core team and volunteering team. Special shout out to Salman - He went out of his way to make each event go smoothly, even when he knew he wouldn't get any official recognition, he showed up! Archana worked relentlessly behind the scenes along with Sanjana and made sure each poster was ready on time (Somehow we always miss her in every group photo). Anusree was the person who takes her responsibilities very seriously - she makes sure its done! Hashim was our first year coordinator, his enthusiasm keeps others engaged - definitely a main character energy, but he was engaged in many other activites, exploring in his own way, so I believe he couldn't give his 100% - but that's fine. Aishwarya was the last person to join the team - quietly she brought her own charm into every design.

![TinkerHub GEC Palakkad 2025 core team](../images/blogs/life-is-good/core-team-25.webp)

At the start, I ran the core team like I was the one who decided everything (it was a bad idea) - Yes, it kept events going but it isn't organic. Core team exists for a reason - everyone have equal role in keeping community running, there is a thing called ownership (I learnt it during winter camp). No one can exhibit their true potential without having the space of their own. To the people running communities - please trust your team and give them space (Don't abandon them though, haha).

I see my leadership tenure in 3 phases. The 2nd phase was bad, I was struck down by a personal issue - I was at my lowest, I even doubted that anyone would listen to me. For 2 months, I had no motivation, I was seriously thinking of just resigning as a lead. Pending events were piling up, some makers were questioning the absence of events. Just after that, college election came... We weren't allowed to run any forms of event for almost 3 weeks. And the worst of all this was that we had to postpone Tink-her-hack - all the preparations we had done until then rendered useless. We had to call for applications again, shortlist them, re-request permission, etc. etc. - it was a mess. But Sreelakshmi (The host) managed it well.

![Portfolio workshop](../images/blogs/life-is-good/portfolio-workshop.webp)

Throughout the whole tenure, numerous events failed (Low participation, technical issues, managemental issues, etc.). Our event count was low. But we weren't event organisers. We were inexperienced. Each activity was a reminder of that.

Finally, Im obliged to talk about the awesome leads, each and every one of them had a different aspiration and perception - each moment spent with them was therapy for me. I got to know how diverse each campus can be. It was an eye-opener. Here are some notable people for me (Please don't feel bad if your name isn't in the list, if I were include this blog wouldn't be enough :))

![Campus Leads Class of 2025](../images/blogs/life-is-good/class-of-25.webp)

**Salha** was a familiar face for me. I never expected to see her in person after 12th. I know her via NSS (mentioned earlier). **Abhijith** - everyone in the campus community and its related area knows about him. His take on community is what I believe everyone should be looking onto. His commitment to things is truly admirable. Being the campus lead of CE Karunagapally, he fit that role perfectly.

![Campus leads together](../images/blogs/life-is-good/nadikar-sangam.webp)

There are many many many more awesome leads - I may have to create seperate blogs for each, haha.

![Giving a talk](../images/blogs/life-is-good/fahad-speech.webp)

As a summary, my one year as a campus lead wasn't smooth and it definitely wasn't full of successes. But the community, the people and the camps truly made it special.

**PS:** I couldn't resist my urge to write this blog - because these are things that hold a special value for me. And I wish everyone get to experience something similar.
